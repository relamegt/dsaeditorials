# Multiplication of Decimals

Multiplication of decimals is a fundamental arithmetic operation frequently tested in quantitative aptitude examinations. It is commonly used in problems involving money, measurements, distance, area, volume, percentages, profit and loss, and data interpretation. Understanding the correct multiplication method helps improve both speed and accuracy in competitive exams.

---

# Core Concept

Multiplying decimal numbers follows the same procedure as multiplying whole numbers. The only additional step is placing the decimal point correctly in the final answer.

### Basic Rule

1. Ignore the decimal points and multiply the numbers as whole numbers.
2. Count the total number of decimal places in both factors.
3. Place the decimal point in the product so that it contains the same total number of decimal places.

### Example

Find:

**2.5 × 1.4**

Multiply as whole numbers.

**25 × 14 = 350**

Total decimal places:

- 2.5 → 1 decimal place
- 1.4 → 1 decimal place

Total = **2 decimal places**

Therefore,

**2.5 × 1.4 = 3.50 = 3.5**

---

# Key Rules

## Rule 1: Ignore the Decimal Point Initially

Multiply the numbers exactly as whole numbers.

### Example

Find:

**3.2 × 4**

Multiply:

**32 × 4 = 128**

There is **1 decimal place** in 3.2.

Therefore,

**3.2 × 4 = 12.8**

---

## Rule 2: Count the Total Decimal Places

The number of decimal places in the answer equals the total decimal places in both factors.

### Example

Find:

**1.25 × 0.4**

Decimal places:

- 1.25 → 2
- 0.4 → 1

Total decimal places = **3**

Multiply:

**125 × 4 = 500**

Place the decimal point three places from the right.

**Answer:** **0.500 = 0.5**

---

## Rule 3: Multiplication by Powers of 10

When multiplying by 10, 100, or 1000, move the decimal point to the right.

| Expression | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 2.45 × 10 | 24.5 |
| 2.45 × 100 | 245 |
| 2.45 × 1000 | 2450 |

---

## Rule 4: Multiplication by 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001

When multiplying by 0.1, 0.01, or 0.001, move the decimal point to the left.

| Expression | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 25 × 0.1 | 2.5 |
| 25 × 0.01 | 0.25 |
| 25 × 0.001 | 0.025 |

---

# Step-by-Step Method

Follow these steps to multiply decimal numbers.

1. Ignore the decimal points.
2. Multiply the numbers as whole numbers.
3. Count the total decimal places in both factors.
4. Place the decimal point in the product.
5. Remove unnecessary trailing zeros if required.

# Standard Question Types

## Type 1: Decimal × Whole Number

### Example

Find:

**0.75 × 4**

### Solution

Multiply:

**75 × 4 = 300**

There are **2 decimal places**.

Therefore,

**0.75 × 4 = 3.00 = 3**

---

## Type 2: Decimal × Decimal

### Example

Find:

**2.4 × 1.5**

### Solution

Multiply:

**24 × 15 = 360**

Decimal places:

- 2.4 → 1
- 1.5 → 1

Total = **2**

Therefore,

**2.4 × 1.5 = 3.60 = 3.6**

---

## Type 3: Multiplication by Powers of 10

### Example

Find:

**4.82 × 100**

### Solution

Move the decimal point two places to the right.

**4.82 → 482**

**Answer:** **482**

---

## Type 4: Word Problems

Use multiplication when:

- The same quantity is repeated.
- A rate is multiplied by a quantity.
- Cost is multiplied by the number of items.
- Length is multiplied by the number of pieces.
- Consumption is multiplied by distance.
- Price is multiplied by quantity.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

A coffee shop uses **0.75 kg** of sugar for one batch of coffee.

If **4 batches** are prepared, how much sugar is required?

### Solution

**0.75 × 4 = 3.00**

**Answer:** **3 kg**

---

## Example 2

An online bookstore sells an e-magazine for **₹2.40** per copy.

A customer purchases **5 copies**.

Find the total cost.

### Solution

**2.40 × 5 = 12.00**

**Answer:** **₹12.00**

---

## Example 3

A runner completes one lap measuring **1.25 miles**.

If she runs **3 laps**, find the total distance covered.

### Solution

**1.25 × 3 = 3.75**

**Answer:** **3.75 miles**

---

## Example 4

Each piece of copper wire measures **0.5 metres**.

If **8 pieces** are used, find the total length.

### Solution

**0.5 × 8 = 4.0**

**Answer:** **4 metres**

---

## Example 5

A delivery van consumes **0.08 litres** of fuel per kilometre.

How much fuel is consumed over **45 kilometres**?

### Solution

Multiply as whole numbers.

**8 × 45 = 360**

There are **2 decimal places**.

Therefore,

**0.08 × 45 = 3.60**

**Answer:** **3.6 litres**

---

## Example 6

A graphic designer applies a scaling factor of **1.45** and then multiplies it by **3.2**.

Find the resulting value.

### Solution

Multiply as whole numbers.

**145 × 32 = 4640**

Decimal places:

- 1.45 → 2
- 3.2 → 1

Total = **3**

Therefore,

**1.45 × 3.2 = 4.640 = 4.64**

**Answer:** **4.64**

---

## Example 7

Each handmade tile weighs **0.65 kg**.

A shipment contains **12 boxes**, with **4 tiles** in each box.

Find the total weight.

### Solution

Number of tiles:

**12 × 4 = 48**

Total weight:

**48 × 0.65 = 31.20**

**Answer:** **31.2 kg**

# Competitive Exam Tricks

### Trick 1

Ignore the decimal points while multiplying.

Place the decimal point only after completing the multiplication.

---

### Trick 2

Estimate the answer before calculating.

Example:

**4.98 × 9.95 ≈ 5 × 10 = 50**

If your answer is far from 50, check your calculation.

---

### Trick 3

When multiplying by powers of 10, simply move the decimal point to the right.

---

### Trick 4

Memorize common decimal products such as:

- 0.5 × 2 = 1
- 0.25 × 4 = 1
- 0.125 × 8 = 1
- 0.75 × 4 = 3

These shortcuts save valuable time during competitive examinations.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to count the total decimal places.
- Placing the decimal point incorrectly.
- Ignoring trailing zeros while counting decimal places.
- Making errors in whole-number multiplication.
- Forgetting to estimate the answer.

---

# Exam Tips

- Multiply the numbers first, then place the decimal point.
- Count decimal places carefully before writing the final answer.
- Estimate the answer to identify calculation mistakes quickly.
- Remember that multiplying by 10, 100, or 1000 shifts the decimal point to the right.
- Practice both direct calculations and word problems.

---

# Summary

Multiplication of decimals follows the same procedure as whole-number multiplication. After multiplying the numbers, place the decimal point according to the total number of decimal places in both factors. Mastering decimal multiplication, powers of 10, estimation techniques, and common shortcut values helps solve quantitative aptitude problems quickly and accurately.
