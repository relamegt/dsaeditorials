# Product Relation (HCF × LCM)

In quant analysis and testing, **Product Relation (HCF × LCM)** is a core pattern area. Mastering these concepts and shortcut rules is essential for solving competitive exam puzzles and verbal tests efficiently.

## Core Concepts and Fundamental Patterns

The **Highest Common Factor (HCF)**, also known as Greatest Common Divisor (GCD), of two or more numbers is the greatest number that divides each of them exactly without leaving any remainder.

### Primary Rules

- **Base Property**: Initial variables map directly to their standard mathematical forms.
- **Transformation Rule**: Operations must follow order limits to prevent calculation errors.

### How to Calculate / Find - Step-by-Step Methodology

To solve problems relating to this topic, follow these structured guidelines:

- **Standard Variation 1: Direct HCF**: Finding the highest common factor. Prime factorization: $HCF(a, b) = \text{product of lowest powers of common primes}$.
- **Standard Variation 2: HCF with different remainders**: Find the greatest number dividing $A, B, C$ leaving remainders $x, y, z \Rightarrow$ Find $\text{HCF}(A-x, B-y, C-z)$.
- **Standard Variation 3: HCF with same remainder (unknown)**: Find the greatest number dividing $A, B, C$ leaving the same remainder in each case $\Rightarrow$ Find $\text{HCF}(|A-B|, |B-C|, |C-A|)$.
- **Standard Variation 4: Minimum rows/boxes packing**: When packing items of sizes $A, B, C$ in equal racks without mixing. First find HCF ($H$), then total racks required $= \frac{A}{H} + \frac{B}{H} + \frac{C}{H}$.
- **For Fractions**: $\text{HCF of Fractions} = \frac{\text{HCF of Numerators}}{\text{LCM of Denominators}}$.

### Practical Calculations

- **Local Bakery Produces Two**: A local bakery produces two types of artisan bread loaves. The product of their codes is 2400, and their Highest Common Factor (H.C.F.) is 20. What is their Least Common Multiple (L.C.M.)?
- *Step-by-Step*: Since Product of two numbers = H.C.F. × L.C.M., we have 2400 = 20 × L.C.M., which gives L.C.M. = 2400 / 20 = 120. (Result: **120**)
- **It Firm Distributes Task**: An IT firm distributes task IDs where two specific IDs have a product of 1800 and their H.C.F. is 15. Determine their L.C.M.
- *Step-by-Step*: Using the relation Product = HCF × LCM, LCM = 1800 / 15 = 120. (Result: **120**)

### Practical Calculations

- **Logistics Audit Two Container**: During a logistics audit, two container batches have an L.C.M. of 450 and an H.C.F. of 15. If one batch number is 75, what is the other batch number?
- *Step-by-Step*: Product of numbers = H.C.F. × L.C.M. Therefore, 75 × x = 15 × 450. Solving gives x = 6750 / 75 = 90. (Result: **90**)
- **Two Employee Seating Rows**: Two employee seating rows have identification numbers whose product is 3600. If their L.C.M. is 300, what is their H.C.F.?
- *Step-by-Step*: H.C.F. = Product of numbers / L.C.M. = 3600 / 300 = 12. (Result: **12**)

### Practical Calculations

- **Retail Chain Stocks Two**: A retail chain stocks two seasonal items whose H.C.F. and L.C.M. are 18 and 378 respectively. If one item code is 54, what is the other item code?
- *Step-by-Step*: Let the other number be x. Then 54 × x = 18 × 378. Solving for x yields x = (18 × 378) / 54 = 126. (Result: **126**)
