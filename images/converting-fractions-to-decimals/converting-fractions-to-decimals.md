# Converting Fractions to Decimals

Converting fractions to decimals is a fundamental arithmetic skill frequently tested in quantitative aptitude examinations. It is used in percentages, ratio and proportion, profit and loss, probability, averages, data interpretation, and many real-life calculations. Understanding different conversion techniques helps solve problems quickly and accurately.

---

# Core Concept

A **fraction** represents a part of a whole and is written in the form:

\[
\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}}
\]

A **decimal** is another way of representing the same value using the base-10 number system.

A fraction can be converted into a decimal by dividing the numerator by the denominator.

\[
\boxed{\text{Decimal}=\frac{\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}}}
\]

---

# Key Rules

### Rule 1: Divide the Numerator by the Denominator

The most common method is long division.

**Example**

\[
\frac34=3\div4=0.75
\]

---

### Rule 2: Convert to an Equivalent Fraction

If the denominator can be converted into **10, 100, 1000**, etc., the decimal can be obtained directly.

**Example**

\[
\frac35=\frac{6}{10}=0.6
\]

---

### Rule 3: Simplify First

Always simplify the fraction before converting whenever possible.

**Example**

\[
\frac{32}{128}
=
\frac14
=
0.25
\]

---

# Types of Decimal Numbers

## 1. Terminating Decimals

A terminating decimal has a finite number of digits after the decimal point.

Examples

\[
\frac12=0.5
\]

\[
\frac58=0.625
\]

\[
\frac{13}{20}=0.65
\]

A fraction produces a terminating decimal if the denominator (in simplest form) has only **2** and/or **5** as its prime factors.

---

## 2. Recurring (Repeating) Decimals

Some fractions produce digits that repeat endlessly.

Examples

\[
\frac13=0.3333\ldots
\]

\[
\frac29=0.2222\ldots
\]

\[
\frac{11}{6}=1.8333\ldots
\]

Recurring decimals are usually represented using a bar notation.

Example

\[
\frac13=0.\overline3
\]

---

# Step-by-Step Method

Follow these steps to convert a fraction into a decimal.

1. Simplify the fraction, if possible.
2. Divide the numerator by the denominator.
3. Continue the division until:

- the remainder becomes zero (terminating decimal), or
- a repeating remainder appears (recurring decimal).

1. Write the decimal answer correctly.

# Standard Question Types

## Type 1: Direct Conversion

Convert the fraction into a decimal using division.

### Example

Convert

\[
\frac34
\]

### Solution

\[
3\div4=0.75
\]

**Answer:** **0.75**

---

## Type 2: Equivalent Fraction Method

Convert the denominator into 10, 100, or 1000.

### Example

Convert

\[
\frac15
\]

### Solution

Multiply numerator and denominator by 2.

\[
\frac15=\frac2{10}=0.2
\]

**Answer:** **0.2**

---

## Type 3: Simplify Before Converting

### Example

Convert

\[
\frac{32}{128}
\]

### Solution

Simplify first.

\[
\frac{32}{128}
=
\frac14
\]

Now convert.

\[
\frac14=0.25
\]

**Answer:** **0.25**

# Competitive Exam Tricks

### Trick 1

If the denominator is **10, 100, or 1000**, simply place the decimal point accordingly.

Example

\[
\frac7{100}=0.07
\]

---

### Trick 2

If the denominator is **2, 4, 5, 8, 20, 25, 50, or 125**, convert it into **10, 100, or 1000** using equivalent fractions for faster calculations.

---

### Trick 3

Memorize commonly used fraction-to-decimal conversions to save time during exams.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to simplify the fraction before converting.
- Dividing the denominator by the numerator instead of the numerator by the denominator.
- Misplacing the decimal point.
- Assuming every fraction produces a terminating decimal.
- Rounding recurring decimals incorrectly when an exact answer is required.

---

# Exam Tips

- Simplify fractions whenever possible before performing division.
- Recognize denominators that can easily be converted to powers of 10.
- Memorize frequently used decimal equivalents such as 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/5, 1/8, and 7/8.
- Practice long division for fractions that do not convert directly.
- Identify whether the decimal is terminating or recurring before writing the final answer.

---

# Summary

Converting fractions to decimals involves dividing the numerator by the denominator or converting the fraction into an equivalent fraction with a denominator of 10, 100, or 1000 whenever possible. A strong understanding of terminating and recurring decimals, along with familiarity with common fraction-to-decimal conversions, helps solve quantitative aptitude questions quickly and accurately in competitive examinations.
