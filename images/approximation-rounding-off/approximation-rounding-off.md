# Approximation & Rounding Off

Approximation and rounding off are important arithmetic techniques used to simplify numbers while keeping them close to their original values. These concepts are frequently tested in quantitative aptitude examinations and are widely used in estimation, measurements, financial calculations, statistics, and data interpretation. Mastering rounding techniques helps perform faster mental calculations and estimate answers efficiently.

---

# Core Concept

**Approximation** means replacing a number with a nearby value that is easier to calculate.

**Rounding off** is a method of approximation in which a number is rounded to a specified place value, such as the nearest whole number, tenth, hundredth, or thousandth.

---

# Key Rules

## Rule 1: Identify the Required Place

Determine the place value to which the number needs to be rounded.

Examples:

- Nearest whole number
- Nearest tenth
- Nearest hundredth
- Nearest thousandth

---

## Rule 2: Look at the Next Digit

After identifying the required place, observe the digit immediately to its right.

- If the digit is **0, 1, 2, 3, or 4**, keep the required digit unchanged.
- If the digit is **5, 6, 7, 8, or 9**, increase the required digit by **1**.

---

## Rule 3: Remove Remaining Digits

After rounding, remove all digits to the right of the required place.

For whole numbers, replace removed digits with zeros if necessary.

---

## Rule 4: Rounding Whole Numbers

### Example

Round **4586** to the nearest hundred.

Hundreds digit = **5**

Next digit = **8**

Since **8 ≥ 5**, increase the hundreds digit by 1.

**Answer:** **4600**

---

# Step-by-Step Method

Follow these steps to round any number.

1. Identify the required place value.
2. Look at the digit immediately to its right.
3. Apply the rounding rule.
4. Remove all remaining digits.
5. Verify that the rounded value is close to the original number.

# Standard Question Types

## Type 1: Round to the Nearest Whole Number

### Example

Round **14.78** to the nearest whole number.

### Solution

Whole number = **14**

Tenths digit = **7**

Since **7 ≥ 5**, increase 14 to 15.

**Answer:** **15**

---

## Type 2: Round to One Decimal Place

### Example

Round **45.612** to one decimal place.

### Solution

Tenths digit = **6**

Hundredths digit = **1**

Since **1 &lt; 5**, keep the tenths digit unchanged.

**Answer:** **45.6**

---

## Type 3: Round to Two Decimal Places

### Example

Round **3.245** to two decimal places.

### Solution

Hundredths digit = **4**

Thousandths digit = **5**

Since **5 ≥ 5**, increase the hundredths digit by 1.

**Answer:** **3.25**

---

## Type 4: Round to Three Decimal Places

### Example

Round **8.5146** to three decimal places.

### Solution

Thousandths digit = **4**

Next digit = **6**

Since **6 ≥ 5**, increase the thousandths digit by 1.

**Answer:** **8.515**

# Approximation for Mental Calculations

Approximation is often used to estimate answers quickly.

### Example

Estimate

**49.8 + 20.3**

Approximate:

- 49.8 ≈ 50
- 20.3 ≈ 20

Estimated answer:

**50 + 20 = 70**

Actual answer:

**70.1**

---

### Example

Estimate

**19.7 × 5.2**

Approximate:

- 19.7 ≈ 20
- 5.2 ≈ 5

Estimated answer:

**20 × 5 = 100**

Actual answer:

**102.44**

# Common Mistakes

- Looking at the wrong digit while rounding.
- Forgetting to increase the required digit when the next digit is 5 or more.
- Rounding more than once during a calculation.
- Ignoring the place value specified in the question.
- Confusing approximation with an exact answer.

---

# Exam Tips

- Read the question carefully to identify the required place value.
- Estimate answers before performing lengthy calculations.
- Use approximation only when the question permits.
- In exact-answer questions, avoid unnecessary rounding until the final step.
- Practice rounding to different decimal places regularly.
