# Mixed HCF & LCM Problems

In quant analysis and testing, **Mixed HCF & LCM Problems** is a core pattern area. Mastering these concepts and shortcut rules is essential for solving competitive exam puzzles and verbal tests efficiently.

## Core Concepts and Fundamental Patterns

The **Highest Common Factor (HCF)**, also known as Greatest Common Divisor (GCD), of two or more numbers is the greatest number that divides each of them exactly without leaving any remainder.

### Primary Rules

- **Base Property**: Initial variables map directly to their standard mathematical forms.
- **Transformation Rule**: Operations must follow order limits to prevent calculation errors.

### How to Calculate / Find - Step-by-Step Methodology

To solve problems relating to this topic, follow these structured guidelines:

- **Standard Variation 1: Direct HCF**: Finding the highest common factor. Prime factorization: $HCF(a, b) = \text{product of lowest powers of common primes}$.
- **Standard Variation 2: HCF with different remainders**: Find the greatest number dividing $A, B, C$ leaving remainders $x, y, z \Rightarrow$ Find $\text{HCF}(A-x, B-y, C-z)$.
- **Standard Variation 3: HCF with same remainder (unknown)**: Find the greatest number dividing $A, B, C$ leaving the same remainder in each case $\Rightarrow$ Find $\text{HCF}(|A-B|, |B-C|, |C-A|)$.
- **Standard Variation 4: Minimum rows/boxes packing**: When packing items of sizes $A, B, C$ in equal racks without mixing. First find HCF ($H$), then total racks required $= \frac{A}{H} + \frac{B}{H} + \frac{C}{H}$.
- **For Fractions**: $\text{HCF of Fractions} = \frac{\text{HCF of Numerators}}{\text{LCM of Denominators}}$.

### Practical Calculations

- **Office Stationery Store Has**: An office stationery store has 24 ballpoint pens and 36 gel pens to pack into identical gift bundles without any pen left over. What is the maximum number of identical bundles that can be created?
- *Step-by-Step*: The maximum number of identical bundles is the H.C.F. of 24 and 36, which is 12. (Result: **12**)
- **Three Digital Display Boards**: Three digital display boards in a corporate park blink their lights at intervals of 4, 6, and 8 seconds respectively. If they blink together right now, after how many seconds will they blink together next?
- *Step-by-Step*: The time they blink together next is the L.C.M. of 4, 6, and 8, which is 24 seconds. (Result: **24**)

## Advanced Patterns and Complex Forms

For higher difficulty levels, variables are nested, requiring secondary substitutions and multi-step mathematical reasoning.

### Analytical Rules

- **Substitution Method**: Complex terms are renamed temporarily to make the main logic visible.
- **Double Expansion**: Factoring or simplifying recursively until the simplest form is reached.

### Practical Calculations

- **Logistics Company Needs Distribute**: A logistics company needs to distribute 45 safety helmets and 75 reflective vests equally among several rescue teams. How many maximum teams can be formed such that each team receives an equal number of each item?
- *Step-by-Step*: The maximum number of teams is given by the H.C.F. of 45 and 75, which is 15. (Result: **15**)
- **Two Runner Colleagues Jog**: Two runner colleagues jog around a circular corporate track, completing one full lap in 12 minutes and 18 minutes respectively. If they start running together from the same point in the same direction, after how many minutes will they meet again at the starting point?
- *Step-by-Step*: The time when they meet again at the start is the L.C.M. of 12 and 18, which is 36 minutes. (Result: **36**)

## Solving Complex Mixed HCF & LCM Problems Scenarios

Applying logical equations to word problems involves isolating unknowns and analyzing constraints.

### Higher-Level Rules

- **System Modeling**: Translate word puzzles into raw equations or logical trees.
- **Verification**: Test the boundary conditions of your output to ensure validity.

### Practical Calculations

- **Product Of Two Positive**: The product of two positive numbers is 2160 and their H.C.F. is 12. Find their L.C.M.
- *Step-by-Step*: Using the property Product of two numbers = H.C.F. × L.C.M., L.C.M. = 2160 / 12 = 180. (Result: **180**)
