# Decimal Place Value

Decimal Place Value is a fundamental arithmetic concept that helps determine the value of each digit based on its position in a decimal number. A strong understanding of place value is essential for comparing decimals, rounding numbers, performing arithmetic operations, and solving quantitative aptitude questions in competitive examinations.

---

# Core Concepts

## What is Decimal Place Value?

The **place value** of a digit is the value it represents because of its position within a number.

For decimal numbers, each place to the **right of the decimal point** represents one-tenth of the place immediately to its left.

### Example

In the number **4.825**:

| Digit | Place | Place Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Ones | 4 |
| 8 | Tenths | 0.8 |
| 2 | Hundredths | 0.02 |
| 5 | Thousandths | 0.005 |

---

# Decimal Place Value Chart

| Position | Decimal Value |
| --- | --- |
| Ones | 1 |
| Tenths | 0.1 |
| Hundredths | 0.01 |
| Thousandths | 0.001 |
| Ten-Thousandths | 0.0001 |
| Hundred-Thousandths | 0.00001 |
| Millionths | 0.000001 |

---

# Key Rules

- The first digit to the right of the decimal point is the **tenths** place.
- The second digit is the **hundredths** place.
- The third digit is the **thousandths** place.
- Every place moving to the right becomes **10 times smaller**.
- Every place moving to the left becomes **10 times larger**.
- The place value of a digit equals:

\[
\text{Digit} \times \text{Place Value}
\]

---

# How to Find the Place Value of a Digit

Follow these steps:

1. Locate the required digit.
2. Count its position from the decimal point.
3. Identify its place (tenths, hundredths, thousandths, etc.).
4. Multiply the digit by its place value.

---

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

A warehouse inventory records the weight of a microchip as **4.825 grams**.

Find the place value of the digit **2**.

### Solution

The digit **2** is the **second digit** after the decimal point.

Therefore, it is in the **hundredths place**.

\[
2 \times 0.01 = 0.02
\]

**Answer:** **0.02**

---

## Example 2

A runner completes a lap in **54.309 seconds**.

Which digit occupies the thousandths place?

### Solution

The digits after the decimal are:

- Tenths → 3
- Hundredths → 0
- Thousandths → 9

**Answer:** **9**

---

## Example 3

An electrical engineer measures a current of **0.076 amperes**.

What is the place value of the digit **7**?

### Solution

The digit **7** is in the hundredths place.

\[
7 \times 0.01 = 0.07
\]

**Answer:** **0.07**

---

## Example 4

A solution has a concentration of **12.345 grams per litre**.

Find the sum of the place values of **3** and **5**.

### Solution

Place value of 3

\[
3 \times 0.1 = 0.3
\]

Place value of 5

\[
5 \times 0.001 = 0.005
\]

Sum

\[
0.3+0.005=0.305
\]

**Answer:** **0.305**

---

# Expanded Form of Decimal Numbers

A decimal number can be written as the sum of the place values of its digits.

### Example

\[
12.345
\]

Expanded form

\[
10+2+0.3+0.04+0.005
\]

# Multiplication and Division by Powers of 10

When multiplying by powers of 10, move the decimal point to the **right**.

| Expression | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 4.56 × 10 | 45.6 |
| 4.56 × 100 | 456 |
| 4.56 × 1000 | 4560 |

When dividing by powers of 10, move the decimal point to the **left**.

| Expression | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 456 ÷ 10 | 45.6 |
| 456 ÷ 100 | 4.56 |
| 456 ÷ 1000 | 0.456 |

---

# Comparing Decimal Numbers

To compare decimals:

1. Compare the whole number part.
2. If equal, compare digits from left to right after the decimal point.

### Example

Compare

\[
0.45 \quad \text{and} \quad 0.405
\]

Write both with equal decimal places:

\[
0.450
\]

\[
0.405
\]

Since

450 &gt; 405
0.45 &gt; 0.405

---

# Rounding Decimals

To round a decimal:

1. Locate the required place.
2. Look at the next digit.
3. If it is **5 or greater**, increase the required digit by 1.
4. Otherwise, keep it unchanged.

### Example

Round **3.467** to the nearest tenth.

The hundredths digit is **6**, so increase the tenths digit.

\[
3.467 \approx 3.5
\]

# Practical Competitive Exam Problems

## Problem 1

A medicine bottle contains **15.428 litres** of solution.

Find the place value of the digit **4**.

### Solution

The digit **4** is in the tenths place.

\[
4 \times 0.1 = 0.4
\]

**Answer:** **0.4**

---

## Problem 2

A rainfall measurement is **8.3046 cm**.

Which digit is in the ten-thousandths place?

### Solution

Digits after the decimal:

- Tenths → 3
- Hundredths → 0
- Thousandths → 4
- Ten-thousandths → 6

**Answer:** **6**

---

## Problem 3

Express **7.082** in expanded form.

### Solution

\[
7+0.08+0.002
\]

**Answer:**

\[
7+0.08+0.002
\]

---

## Problem 4

Multiply **5.238** by **100**.

### Solution

Move the decimal point two places to the right.

\[
5.238\times100=523.8
\]

**Answer:** **523.8**

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing a digit with its place value.
- Counting decimal places incorrectly.
- Moving the decimal point in the wrong direction when multiplying or dividing by powers of 10.
- Comparing decimals without equalizing decimal places.
- Forgetting that each place to the right is one-tenth of the previous place.

---

# Exam Tips

- Memorize the decimal place value chart.
- Count decimal places carefully.
- Use trailing zeros when comparing decimals.
- Remember that moving the decimal point:
- Right → Multiply by powers of 10
- Left → Divide by powers of 10
- Always identify the position of the digit before calculating its place value.
