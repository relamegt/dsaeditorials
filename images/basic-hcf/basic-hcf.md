# Basic HCF

In quant analysis and testing, **Basic HCF** is a core pattern area. Mastering these concepts and shortcut rules is essential for solving competitive exam puzzles and verbal tests efficiently.

## Core Concepts and Fundamental Patterns

The **Highest Common Factor (HCF)**, also known as Greatest Common Divisor (GCD), of two or more numbers is the greatest number that divides each of them exactly without leaving any remainder.

### Primary Rules

- **Base Property**: Initial variables map directly to their standard mathematical forms.
- **Transformation Rule**: Operations must follow order limits to prevent calculation errors.

### How to Calculate / Find - Step-by-Step Methodology

To solve problems relating to this topic, follow these structured guidelines:

- **Standard Variation 1: Direct HCF**: Finding the highest common factor. Prime factorization: $HCF(a, b) = \text{product of lowest powers of common primes}$.
- **Standard Variation 2: HCF with different remainders**: Find the greatest number dividing $A, B, C$ leaving remainders $x, y, z \Rightarrow$ Find $\text{HCF}(A-x, B-y, C-z)$.
- **Standard Variation 3: HCF with same remainder (unknown)**: Find the greatest number dividing $A, B, C$ leaving the same remainder in each case $\Rightarrow$ Find $\text{HCF}(|A-B|, |B-C|, |C-A|)$.
- **Standard Variation 4: Minimum rows/boxes packing**: When packing items of sizes $A, B, C$ in equal racks without mixing. First find HCF ($H$), then total racks required $= \frac{A}{H} + \frac{B}{H} + \frac{C}{H}$.
- **For Fractions**: $\text{HCF of Fractions} = \frac{\text{HCF of Numerators}}{\text{LCM of Denominators}}$.

### Practical Calculations

- **Baker Has Chocolate Cookies**: A baker has 24 chocolate cookies and 36 vanilla cookies. He wants to pack them into identical gift boxes with no cookies left over, such that each box has the maximum possible number of chocolate cookies and vanilla cookies. How many chocolate cookies will be in each box?
- *Step-by-Step*: The maximum number of identical boxes is the HCF of 24 and 36, which is 12. Each box will contain 24 / 12 = 2 chocolate cookies. (Result: **2**)
- **It Company Distributes Promotional**: An IT company distributes promotional kits to new hires. There are 45 branded notebooks and 75 pens. Every new hire receives the exact same combination of items with nothing left over. What is the maximum number of new hires who can receive these kits?
- *Step-by-Step*: The maximum number of kits is the HCF of 45 and 75. Prime factorization gives 45 = 3^2 × 5 and 75 = 3 × 5^2, so HCF = 3 × 5 = 15. (Result: **15**)

## Advanced Patterns and Complex Forms

For higher difficulty levels, variables are nested, requiring secondary substitutions and multi-step mathematical reasoning.

### Analytical Rules

- **Substitution Method**: Complex terms are renamed temporarily to make the main logic visible.
- **Double Expansion**: Factoring or simplifying recursively until the simplest form is reached.

### Practical Calculations

- **Logistics Firm Needs Stack**: A logistics firm needs to stack 42 red containers and 56 blue containers in rows of equal length, with each row containing only one color. What is the maximum number of containers that can be placed in each row?
- *Step-by-Step*: The maximum number of containers per row is the HCF of 42 and 56. HCF(42, 56) = 14. (Result: **14**)
- **Florist Prepares Bouquets Using**: A florist prepares bouquets using 60 roses and 84 orchids. Each bouquet must have the same number of roses and the same number of orchids. What is the greatest number of identical bouquets that can be made?
- *Step-by-Step*: The greatest number of identical bouquets is the HCF of 60 and 84. HCF(60, 84) = 12. (Result: **12**)

## Solving Complex Basic HCF Scenarios

Applying logical equations to word problems involves isolating unknowns and analyzing constraints.

### Higher-Level Rules

- **System Modeling**: Translate word puzzles into raw equations or logical trees.
- **Verification**: Test the boundary conditions of your output to ensure validity.

### Practical Calculations

- **Three Rolls Of Electrical**: Three rolls of electrical wire measuring 105 meters, 140 meters, and 175 meters are to be cut into equal pieces without any waste. What is the length of the longest possible piece?
- *Step-by-Step*: The length of the longest piece is the HCF of 105, 140, and 175. HCF(105, 140, 175) = 35. (Result: **35 meters**)

## Practical Application Scenarios: Logical Problem Solving

- **Rohan** is writing a simulation script for: *"A hardware store manager wants to arrange 96 iron rods and 120 copper rods in racks such that every rack has the same number of rods and all racks contain rods of only one metal. What is the minimum total number of racks required?"*

  He runs the loop checks to output: Max rods per rack = HCF(96, 120) = 24. Total racks = (96/24) + (120/24) = 4 + 5 = 9.

  She analyzes grammatical relationships to select: Subtract the respective remainders: 130 - 6 = 124, 235 - 7 = 228, 305 - 9 = 296. The required number is the HCF of 124, 228, and 296, which is 4.
