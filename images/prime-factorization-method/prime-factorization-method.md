# Prime Factorization Method

In quant analysis and testing, **Prime Factorization Method** is a core pattern area. Mastering these concepts and shortcut rules is essential for solving competitive exam puzzles and verbal tests efficiently.

## Core Concepts and Fundamental Patterns

The **Prime Factorization Method** is the process of breaking down a composite number into a product of prime numbers. This is the foundational arithmetic technique for finding divisors, HCF, LCM, and total factors.

### Primary Rules

- **Base Property**: Initial variables map directly to their standard mathematical forms.
- **Transformation Rule**: Operations must follow order limits to prevent calculation errors.

### How to Calculate / Find - Step-by-Step Methodology

To solve problems relating to this topic, follow these structured guidelines:

- **Step 1: Divide by Primes**: Start dividing the number by the smallest prime (2, 3, 5, etc.) that divides it exactly.
- **Step 2: Continue Division**: Divide the resulting quotient by prime numbers until the final quotient becomes 1.
- **Step 3: Write in Exponent Form**: Collect the prime factors and write them with exponents (e.g., $72 = 2^3 \times 3^2$).
- **Rule for HCF**: For HCF, take the minimum exponents of common primes.
- **Rule for LCM**: For LCM, take the maximum exponents of all primes present.

### Practical Calculations

- **Artisan Makes Two Types**: An artisan makes two types of ceramic tiles. Tile type A is produced in batches of 18 and tile type B in batches of 24. What is the smallest total number of tiles of each type the artisan must produce so that the batches can be packed into equal-sized boxes without any leftover tiles?
- *Step-by-Step*: Prime factorizations are 18 = 2 * 3^2 and 24 = 2^3 * 3. The L.C.M. is 2^3 * 3^2 = 72. (Result: **72**)
- **Bakery Packages Muffins Boxes**: A bakery packages muffins in boxes of 12 and brownies in boxes of 16. If a customer wants to buy the exact same number of muffins and brownies, what is the minimum number of muffins they must purchase?
- *Step-by-Step*: Prime factorizations are 12 = 2^2 * 3 and 16 = 2^4. The L.C.M. is 2^4 * 3 = 48. (Result: **48**)

## Advanced Patterns and Complex Forms

For higher difficulty levels, variables are nested, requiring secondary substitutions and multi-step mathematical reasoning.

### Analytical Rules

- **Substitution Method**: Complex terms are renamed temporarily to make the main logic visible.

### Practical Calculations

- **Two Electronic Notice Boards**: Two electronic notice boards flash their lights at intervals of 15 seconds and 20 seconds respectively. If they flash together right now, after how many seconds will they next flash together?
- *Step-by-Step*: Prime factorizations are 15 = 3 * 5 and 20 = 2^2 * 5. The L.C.M. is 2^2 * 3 * 5 = 60. (Result: **60**)
- **Florist Has Roses And**: A florist has 30 roses and 42 orchids. She wants to arrange them into identical floral bouquets with no flowers left over. What is the maximum number of identical bouquets she can make?
- *Step-by-Step*: Prime factorizations are 30 = 2 * 3 * 5 and 42 = 2 * 3 * 7. The H.C.F. is 2 * 3 = 6. (Result: **6**)

- *Step-by-Step*: Prime factorizations are 24 = 2^3 * 3, 36 = 2^2 * 3^2, and 40 = 2^3 * 5. L.C.M. is 2^3 * 3^2 * 5 = 360 minutes,. (Result: **240 minutes**)
